Reflection

Written after the Problem 9 run, with the expected plans (Problem 6) next to what the audit trail shows. Final answers: where several were possible, these are the recommended ones.

1 · How did the agents perform on each ticket?

Overall: the agents got the facts and the rules right but spent too much getting there. Across all three tickets, every number in every report and draft matches the database (0 in Aisle B, $840 three days overdue, $2,400 due in 2 days, 8 of 20 hoodies, $22/$58, 62.1%, 5-day lead). No agent invented a price, date or approval, every payment paused for a human, and nothing was "sent". The weak spot was efficiency: 25 successful hand-offs, 17 refused, 95 model calls and about 272k tokens for three small tickets. Most of that was the same fact being re-verified by a second and third agent.

A−Ticket 101 · Bulldog tee

The right first call (Inventory), the right specialists (Facilities correctly skipped), and the key insight: the tee can't be reprinted until invoice 501 is paid, because of the unpaid-invoice shipping rule. The draft to Tauhid is honest and kind, with no false date. Points off for chatter: Inventory ran 3 times and Accounting 3 times, and Accounting ran a cost/price lookup the ticket didn't need. It was the cheapest run (74k tokens) and the closest to the plan.

B+Ticket 102 · Rent notice

Exactly the right instinct: verify the email against the lease before any money. Facilities even noticed the email never stated an amount, a sharp catch a real fraud check would want. Accounting paid from the lease amount, not the email, and did the cross-ticket budget math ($160 left if both bills are paid). Points off because the same lease was verified 4 times and Customer Service was called twice for a landlord draft the plan didn't call for. That draft is nice to have, but 88k tokens for "confirm and queue rent" is a lot.

BTicket 103 · Bulk hoodie discount

The hardest ticket and the most cautious, careful answer: 8 on hand / short 12, no discount promised without authority, the reprint is blocked by the same vendor invoice, and the reprint can't be funded after the other two bills. Marking it blocked was a mature call. But it pulled in all five agents, refused 9 delegations at the guards, and cost 110k tokens. The Boss also asked Inventory before Accounting, the opposite of the plan, though that didn't change the result.

What the grades rest on

  • Grounding: excellent. Every claim traces to a tool result in audit_trail.json.
  • Rule-following: excellent. Desk date used, lead times from vendors, unpaid-invoice hold applied, human approval on every payment, no negative balance possible, drafts only.
  • Efficiency: weak. "Verify everything" in the shared rules plus "full connectivity" made specialists re-ask each other for facts the caller already had. The guards (no bounce-back, depth 2, 12 per run) did real work: 17 asks were refused.
  • Resolution: incomplete, by design. With no human signature (your call for this run), no ticket could honestly be closed. The agents said so instead of pretending.

The pattern: the Boss's first move matched the plan 2 of 3 times, and the set of specialists matched on 101 and was close on 102. Divergence almost always came from specialists delegating sideways: Accounting → Facilities, Customer Service → everyone. The plan only modeled the Boss's calls. Full connectivity is powerful, but each specialist treats "ask a teammate" as cheap insurance, and that multiplies cost.

What I'd change next: (1) let delegated requests carry the facts already verified ("Inventory confirmed 0 in Aisle B at 23:49 — do not re-check"); (2) give specialists a smaller delegation budget than the Boss (for example 1 each); (3) say in the shop rules that "a fact a teammate already returned in this run counts as verified."

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools?

Ticket 102 (rent) as one agent
It's a straight line: read the notice → get_lease_rent_and_cash → list_payments → pay_rent (human approval). There's one decision and no trade-offs between departments. One agent with those 4 tools would make about 4 tool calls and one approval request. The team made 31 model calls, verified the lease 4 times and used 88k tokens. Separation of duties (only someone allowed to pay can pay) is already enforced by the approval gate and tool permissions, not by having a separate Accounting persona.

Why this one: 102 shows the biggest gap between the work needed and the work done, and the risk (paying a fake landlord) is handled by tools and the approval gate rather than by more agents. A team still earns its cost on tickets like 103, where pricing, stock and cash pull against each other.

4 · Three new problems this team could solve with the tools already built

1. "Do you have a Crest Mug?": restock any out-of-stock item
CC-MUG-CREST is at 0 in Aisle D today. Inventory: check_stock_by_sku_and_size → list_vendors (Elm City Gifts, mugs, 3-day lead) → list_open_invoices(vendor 2) finds none, so this vendor can ship. Customer Service drafts "back in about 3 days once ordered". It's a real gap in today's data, and it exercises the vendor-hold rule in the opposite direction from ticket 101.
2. "Can we pay everything due this week?": a cash-runway check
Accounting: get_cash_balances + list_open_invoices + get_lease_rent_and_cash + list_payments → $3,400 − $840 − $2,400 = $160, then rank by urgency (invoice overdue 3 days vs rent due in 2). Queue the payments in that order for human signature. Ticket 103's note shows the agents already reason this way.
3. A suspicious second rent email ("rent is $2,900, pay today")
Facilities: get_ticket + get_lease_rent_and_cash + list_payments → amount mismatch ($2,900 vs $2,400) and, if this month is already paid, a payment on record, so it's flagged as a possible scam. pay_rent pays only the lease amount and refuses to pay ahead, and CS drafts a polite "please confirm". It shows the tools protect money even when an email lies.

5 · Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

1. Actually ordering the reprint (purchasing and receiving stock)
Why not today: agents can see a vendor and its lead time, but there's no purchase-order table, no vendor price list, and no way to receive stock. Ticket 103's 12 hoodies can't be ordered or costed.
Would need: tables purchase_orders and vendor_prices; MCP tools get_vendor_quote(vendor, sku, qty), create_purchase_order(...) (human approval, with a cash check against committed spend), and receive_stock(po_id) (adds to inventory, creates the vendor invoice). Agent: a Procurement agent, or extend Inventory, which plans reorders against lead times and open-invoice holds.
2. Approving and closing the bulk sale (pricing policy and revenue)
Why not today: there's no discount policy, no orders or sales table, and "cash only goes out", so the team could only say "a human must decide". Ticket 103 can't finish.
Would need: a pricing_rules table (for example max 15% off at 20+ units, margin floor 40%); tools quote_price(sku, qty), approve_price_override(...) (human approval above policy), reserve_stock(...) and record_sale(...), which adds revenue to cash. Agent: a Sales / Pricing agent that proposes a quote inside policy and escalates exceptions.
3. Really answering customers and the landlord (and reading the inbox)
Why not today: by rule, drafts stay on the board. There's no email tool, no contact table (the lease has no landlord email to verify a sender), and tickets have to be typed in.
Would need: tables contacts and messages; tools read_inbox() (turns emails into tickets), verify_sender(email, contact), send_message(draft_id) (gated: a human clicks Send on the dashboard). Agent: a Communications agent that triages the inbox and flags phishing. Your Outlook MCP connector could be the transport.
